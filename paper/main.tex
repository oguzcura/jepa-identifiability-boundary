\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The formal identifiability guarantee for Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architectures (JEPAs) is Gaussian-bound: \citet{klindt2026lejepa} prove in
Lean~4 that a LeJEPA objective with sketched Gaussian regularization (SIGReg)
recovers latent states linearly (up to rotation) \emph{if and only if} the
latents are Gaussian under a stationary OU transition. Real language,
perception, and world states are neither Gaussian nor continuous --- they are
categorical, rule-governed, and discrete. This paper maps \emph{how and where}
identifiability breaks as the latent regime leaves the theorem's boundary,
using the theorem's own instruments: procedurally generated worlds with
\emph{known} ground-truth latents and linear-probe readouts. We build a ladder
of worlds from continuous Gaussian (OU) through discretized Gaussian,
Markov-chain categories, a rule-governed vowel-harmony grammar, and a Turkish
orthography (caret-rule) lexicon, and train four objectives (LeJEPA/SIGReg,
input-space reconstruction, InfoNCE, and VICReg) under a frozen,
pre-registered protocol with append-only amendments (A1--A6). Four findings,
all pre-registered, all reported honestly: (i)~the theorem's continuous
signature reproduces (gate passes at the Gaussian shape); (ii)~recovery
collapses with state count, but a genuinely discrete latent is recovered no
worse than a moment-matched continuous latent read out through the same number
of bins --- discreteness of the \emph{generator} is not a new failure mode
beyond discreteness of the \emph{readout} (H1a, same-metric null);
(iii)~compositional rule structure produces a real, JEPA-specific recovery
advantage ($+0.11$ over a marginal-matched unstructured control, 5/5 seeds)
that is robust to the control construction but below the pre-registered
effect-size bar (H1b, honest null); and (iv)~SIGReg's Gaussian forcing does not
measurably blur discrete clusters under the pre-registered metric (H2, honest
null), though the predicted direction appears under a chance-adjusted
instrument. A power analysis shows the frozen bars were detectable at $n{=}5$
(minimum detectable effect $0.081$), so the nulls are genuine sub-bar effects,
not underpowered noise. We also introduce a \emph{decoupling index} ---
prediction quality minus recovery on known ground truth --- which shows LeJEPA
consistently predicts better than stage-average while recovering worse
($d{=}{+}1.1$ to $+1.5$), a signature the non-predictive objectives mirror in
the opposite sign.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and the theorem's boundary}
\label{sec:intro}
The central question for self-supervised world models is identifiability: when
does an embedding recover the \emph{true} latent state of the world, rather
than merely a statistically convenient code? For Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architectures \citep{lecun2022path,assran2023ijepa}, the sharpest formal answer
to date is \citet{klindt2026lejepa}: under stationary OU dynamics and a
sketched Gaussian regularizer (SIGReg), a LeJEPA objective recovers the latent
state \emph{linearly} (up to rotation) if and only if the latents are Gaussian
(their Thm.~5.1, verified in Lean~4 with zero axioms). Their Fig.~4b gives the
empirical signature on the generalized-normal family: linear recovery peaks at
the Gaussian shape ($\alpha=2$) and degrades on both sides toward heavy tails
and uniform.

Two facts make the boundary scientifically live. First, the theorem's own
assumptions --- Gaussian, continuous, stationary-OU --- exclude the regime in
which real data actually lives: language, perception, and world states are
categorical, rule-governed, and discrete. Whether identifiability \emph{breaks
gracefully or collapses} below the boundary is an empirical question that the
formal result, by construction, cannot answer. Second, the continuous
extension is already being mapped by adjacent work
(\citet{arya2026,controlledWM2026}; see Sec.~\ref{sec:related}), but the
discrete generative regime --- categorical latents with known ground truth ---
remains untested anywhere in the prior literature (verified 2026-09-06; a
handful of \emph{token}-discrete and symbolic-predictor systems exist, none of
which measures recovery of known discrete generative latents; see
Sec.~\ref{sec:related}).

\paragraph{This paper.}
We map the boundary empirically with the theorem's own instruments. We build a
ladder of procedurally generated worlds with known ground-truth latents
(Sec.~\ref{sec:worlds}), train the theorem's objective family
(Sec.~\ref{sec:objectives}), and measure linear recovery exactly as the theorem
does (Sec.~\ref{sec:readouts}). Every hypothesis, decision rule, world, and
effect-size bar was frozen \emph{before} the runs it governs, in an
append-only pre-registration with six amendments (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}); the
amendments themselves document where our first instruments were wrong and were
corrected (A4, A6). We report negative and null findings as first-class
results. All computation ran locally at zero financial cost (single RTX~4070,
8~GB), 672 deterministic-seed cells, 0 errors.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item The first discrete ladder (continuous $\to$ binned $\to$ categorical
$\to$ rule grammar $\to$ orthography lexicon) with known latents and the
theorem's readouts, under a frozen pre-registered protocol.
\item A same-metric identifiability instrument for discrete worlds, and the
finding that genuine discreteness adds little beyond readout quantization:
categorical states recover about as well as equiprobable bins of a
moment-matched continuous twin (H1a null under the corrected instrument).
\item Evidence that compositional rule structure helps \emph{only} the
predictive objective --- LeJEPA gains $+0.11$ accuracy over an unstructured
control with identical per-dimension marginals, robust across control
constructions and 5/5 seeds, but below the pre-registered bar.
\item The decoupling index: LeJEPA's prediction--recovery gap is large and
consistently positive ($+1.1$ to $+1.5$ stage-z), quantifying
``predicting-without-understanding'' on known ground truth.
\item A self-audit (A6) of our own instruments, demonstrating the
pre-registration machinery working as intended: an initial ``partial pass''
(H1a at $S{=}10$) was traced to metric mixing (accuracy vs.\ $R^2$) and
re-scored as a null on the correct same-metric comparison.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Identifiability theory for self-supervised learning.}
The formal anchor is \citet{klindt2026lejepa} (Gaussian necessary and
sufficient for linear identifiability of LeJEPA with SIGReg, Lean-4 verified).
\citet{controlledWM2026} extend the theory to action-conditioned world models
with joint representation--transition identifiability, still under Gaussian
latents; the discrete controlled case is the natural residue.
\citet{interventionalCRL2022} establish the founding premise of causal
representation learning (latent factors identifiable given perfect
interventions); our worlds are non-interventional, and we claim no novelty in
that direction.

\paragraph{Discrete and symbolic JEPA variants.}
Several systems quantize \emph{encoder outputs} or induce \emph{symbolic
dynamics}, but none measures recovery of known discrete \emph{generative}
latents: \citet{discretejepa2025} discretize tokens for reasoning;
\citet{mcjepa2026} interpret JEPA as a hidden Markov model and add a learned
transition matrix; \citet{sjepa2026} induce hybrid symbolic--neural predictors;
\citet{rijepa2026} inject rules into JEPA; \citet{aimprobing2026} probe
continuous video encoders for emergent discrete structure. \citet{jepaparadox2026}
analyze the geometry of linguistic alternatives underlying why deterministic
latent prediction struggles on text. All are adjacent;
our contribution is the measurement instrument, not a new objective.

\paragraph{Non-Gaussian and dynamics-focused world models.}
\citet{arya2026} remove Gaussianity but stay in continuous physical systems
(no categorical generative latents). \citet{nogaussian2026} replace SIGReg with
contrastive inverse dynamics --- directly relevant to our H2. \citet{phylatent2026}
taxonomize collapse modes; \citet{uwmjepa2026b,causaljepa2026,dsge2026} study
belief-space or counterfactual world modeling. \citet{leworldmodel2026} is the
practical action-conditioned JEPA ancestor. None tests the discrete generative
regime with known ground truth.

\section{Protocol and pre-registration}
\label{sec:protocol}
The protocol was frozen before any
grid run (SHA-256 hash recorded in the repository) and extended only by
append-only amendments A1--A6:\footnote{The frozen pre-registration document
and its amendment log are in the public repository,
\url{https://github.com/oguzcura/jepa-identifiability-boundary}
(pre-registration document and append-only amendment log in the
repository root).}
A1 (technical clarifications), A2 (nonlinear mixing, dim 8, recon as
one-hot-MSE control), A3 (spiral mixing + SIGReg $\lambda{=}10$; official gate
config), A4 (withdrawal of the invalid L3-v1 world; L3/L4 v2 redesign;
entropy-matched L2m control), A5 (VICReg as the fourth objective; L2t
moment-matched continuous twins; the decoupling index), and A6 (the self-audit:
same-metric H1a scoring, marginal-matched L2mH control for H1b, AMI-based H2
scoring, and the power analysis reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:power}).
All decision rules below are quoted from the frozen text; the frozen
effect-size bars were \emph{not} moved in any amendment.

The discipline matters because this study's honest outputs are mostly nulls,
and nulls are only interpretable if the bars were fixed in advance, the
instruments were validated, and the study was powered. Sec.~\ref{sec:power}
shows the third condition held.

Every world is a paired-view generator $(x,x')$ with known $z$ and a
controllable ``discreteness dial.'' L0 is the theorem's own continuous regime;
L1 (binned Gaussian, $z_K$) is the continuous$\to$discrete bridge; L2 is a
genuinely categorical Markov chain; L3 v2 is a vowel-harmony grammar whose
latents obey a cross-position rule (suffix front/back must match the stem
class); L4 v2 is a curated Turkish lexicon where the latent is
caret-required-ness with a real negative class. Two design rules, imposed after
the A4 audit, hold for all discrete worlds: (i)~the latent must have genuine
variance (checked by empirical entropy), and (ii)~$z$ must never be a literal
column of $x$ (checked by linear-recoverability probes). L2t is a continuous
AR(1) twin of L2 with matched observed moments (R1/A5) whose only difference is
the support of $z$; L2mH (A6) matches L3's per-dimension marginal structure
without the rule. Table~\ref{tab:worlds} lists the ladder; full generator
code, seeded, is in the repository.

\section{Objectives and readouts}
\label{sec:objectives}
All objectives train on identical paired views $(x,x')$ with shared-MLP
encoders (equal capacity):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{LeJEPA/SIGReg} (\texttt{jepa}) --- the theorem's objective:
latent prediction with SIGReg sketched-Gaussian regularization
($\lambda{=}10$ under A3);
\item \textbf{InfoNCE contrastive} (\texttt{contrastive}) --- alignment with
$\tau{=}0.3$ (A3), the non-Gaussian-pressured alignment objective;
\item \textbf{Input-space reconstruction} (\texttt{recon}) --- autoencoder
control (no latent objective at all);
\item \textbf{VICReg} (\texttt{vicreg}; introduced in amendment~A5) --- variance--invariance--
covariance ($25/25/1$, Bardes-style), the theorem's third Fig-4b objective.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Readouts.} \label{sec:readouts}
On continuous worlds: ridge linear $R^2$ of $z$ from frozen embeddings $h$
(per-dim + mean), CCA, and a 2-layer MLP control probe. On discrete worlds:
per-dimension linear-probe top-1 accuracy (mean over dims, chance floor
$1/S$), adjusted mutual information, and KMeans cluster purity; plus collapse
metrics (effective rank) and the prediction loss. The A6 audit established the
metric discipline used throughout: comparisons between a discrete arm and its
continuous twin must hold the \emph{readout} fixed (same-metric: accuracy vs.\
accuracy on the twin's equiprobable $z_K$ bins), never compare accuracy against
$R^2$ on different scales. All readouts are model-agnostic and deterministic
seeded. $n{=}5$ seeds per reported cell.

\section{Reproduction: the sanity gate (H0)}
\label{sec:gate}
\textbf{Official gate: PASS (A3 configuration).} On the L0 $\alpha$-sweep
(2-D spiral mixing, $\lambda{=}10$), LeJEPA ridge $R^2$ rises monotonically
from the heavy-tail region ($0.043$ at $\alpha{=}1/8$) to a peak at the
Gaussian shape ($\mathbf{0.851}$ at $\alpha{=}2$, $n{=}3$ seeds) and decays
toward the uniform extreme ($0.609$ at $\alpha{=}32$). InfoNCE rises to a broad plateau across the Gaussian region
($0.965$ at $\alpha{=}2$) with no sharp peak --- its sweep maximum ($0.983$)
lies at the uniform extreme, so the $\alpha{=}2$ maximum is specific to the
SIGReg objective. This reproduces Fig.~4b with the theorem's own objective
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gate}).
\begin{figure}[ht]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{../figures/F1_l0_fig4b.png}
\caption{L0 $\alpha$-sweep, ridge $R^2$ vs.\ gennorm shape. LeJEPA/SIGReg
peaks at the Gaussian shape ($\alpha{=}2$, $0.851$) and decays toward uniform;
VICReg (stage 6) is flat ($0.975$--$0.987$) --- whitening without

\label{fig:gate}
\end{figure} \emph{Honest negative retained:} the original A2 configuration

disk).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{L1: the continuous$\to$discrete bridge}
\label{sec:l1}
Recovery of the binned latent collapses monotonically with the number of bins
(LeJEPA accuracy $0.899 \to 0.202$ as $K{:}2\to16$, $n{=}10$), while the
underlying continuous latent stays linearly recoverable at every $K$
(ridge $R^2 = 0.738$ flat, $n{=}10$). The bridge quantifies exactly what
discretization costs a linear probe: the bin index is a nonlinear function of
a linearly recovered continuous code. This is the theorem-consistent baseline
against which genuine discreteness (L2) must be compared.

\subsection{H1a: is genuine discreteness worse than quantization? (same-metric null)}
\label{sec:h1a}
The pre-registered question: do \emph{genuinely categorical} latents (L2,
Markov chain) break recovery more than any moment-matched continuous analog?
A5 built L2t, a continuous AR(1) twin of L2 with matched per-dimension
observed moments and persistence; the only difference is the support of $z$.

The A6 audit found that the first scoring of this test mixed metrics: it
compared L2's \emph{accuracy} (chance floor $1/S$) against the twin's
\emph{ridge $R^2$} (chance 0) --- two different scales --- which produced a
spurious-looking $>0.3$ gap at $S{=}10$ (e.g., $0.456$ vs.\ $0.943$). Under the
correct same-metric comparison --- L2's categorical accuracy against L2t's
accuracy at recovering its own equiprobable $S$ bins ($z_K$) --- the gap
collapses (Table~\ref{tab:h1a}):

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
A genuinely discrete latent is recovered \emph{no worse} than a continuous
latent of identical moments read out through the same number of classes. The
one descriptive counter-directional cell --- LeJEPA at $S{=}10$, $d{=}8$
($0.456$ vs.\ $0.527$) --- is the largest gap against LeJEPA anywhere in the
table, yet its paired confidence interval includes zero and it sits far below
the frozen $0.3$ bar, so it is consistent with the null rather than evidence
against it. The
collapse with state count (L1 and L2 both fall steeply as $K$ or $S$ grows) is
therefore a property of the \emph{discrete readout}, not of the discrete
\emph{generator}. This is the theorem's boundary behaving exactly as its
continuity argument predicts: the model recovers the continuous code linearly,
and any nonlinear quantization of that code is what the linear probe cannot
see. The practical implication is negative for a hoped-for ``discreteness
cliff'': categorical generative latents do not, by themselves, create a new
failure mode beyond quantization.

\subsection{H1b: does rule structure buy identifiability? (honest null, directional)}
\label{sec:h1b}
The pre-registered claim: rule-governed worlds (L3) recover \emph{better} than
unstructured categories at matched statistics --- composition itself is a
resource. A4's first control (L2m, homogeneous $S{=}3$) matched only the
\emph{mean} marginal entropy; the A6 audit showed the world it paired against
had 27 joint states vs.\ L3's 8, so the control was state-space mismatched. A6
therefore added \textbf{L2mH}: an unstructured L2 with per-dimension
$S{=}(2,4,4)$, reproducing L3's per-dimension marginal entropies
$[1,2,2]$ and its per-dimension chance floors ($0.5,0.25,0.25$) exactly, with
independent dims and no rule (joint $H{=}5$ bits, 32 states --- \emph{harder}
than L3, biasing \emph{against} our claim). Stage 7 ran 30 fresh cells
(3 models $\times$ 5 seeds $\times$ \{L2mH, L2mS2\}) paired with the existing
stage-4 L3 rows at identical hyperparameters (Table~\ref{tab:h1b},
Fig.~\ref{fig:h1b}).

\paragraph{Where the effect lives.}
Per-dimension readouts (5-seed means) show the stem class --- the
surface-visible latent --- is recovered equally by all three objectives
(LeJEPA $0.876$, recon $0.914$, contrastive $0.929$). LeJEPA's entire advantage
sits in the two \emph{rule-governed} 4-way affix dims ($0.947$, $0.932$ vs.\
recon $0.758$, $0.752$ and contrastive $0.735$, $0.751$). Composition helps
exactly where the harmony rule constrains the suffix vowel --- and it helps
only the objective that must \emph{predict} under that rule.

\subsection{L4: Turkish caret rule, and VICReg}
\label{sec:l4}
L4 v2 (matched negative class, surface-only observations) ran clean
(15/15, 0 errors): LeJEPA accuracy $0.705$, recon $0.772$, contrastive
$0.792$ ($n{=}5$) --- far below the v1 (invalid) ceiling, i.e., the world
discriminates. VICReg (A5), the theorem's third Fig-4b objective, shows
\textbf{no sharp $\alpha{=}2$ peak} on L0: recovery rises to a plateau
($0.987$ at $\alpha{=}2$) and stays flat through the uniform extreme ($0.975$
at $\alpha{=}32$) --- its variance hinge whitens but does not Gaussianize, so
bounded uniform latents stay recoverable. On the discrete ladder VICReg
mirrors the non-predictive objectives: no structure benefit at L3 ($0.714$ vs.\
L2m $0.881$, $\Delta{-}0.167$), steep L2 collapse ($0.840\to0.452$,
$S{:}3\to10$), L4 $0.730$ (descriptive only, no H-test, per A5).

\subsection{H2: does SIGReg blur discrete clusters? (honest null under AMI)}
\label{sec:h2}
H2 was insurance: SIGReg's Gaussian forcing should measurably blur discrete
clusters, making recon's cluster purity exceed LeJEPA's by $>0.1$. The frozen
metric (KMeans purity) sits near its floor for \emph{both} models on
multi-dimensional categorical embeddings and cannot register the effect
($|\text{gap}| \le 0.04$, sign-inconsistent). A6 re-scored on adjusted mutual
information, the chance-adjusted probe-based instrument: the predicted
direction appears in all 9 arms (recon AMI $>$ jepa AMI), and the L2m control
arm of stage 4 clears the frozen bar ($+0.106$, CI $[0.043,0.169]$), but the
remaining arms are below it (L2 S-dial $+0.005$--$+0.045$; L4 $+0.045$ ns; L3
$+0.131$ ns, wide CI). \textbf{H2: honest null} --- the mechanism is
directionally present but does not meet the pre-registered bar; consistent
with \citet{nogaussian2026}, which motivates replacing SIGReg's Gaussian
prescription.

\subsection{The decoupling index: predicting without understanding}
\label{sec:decoupling}
A5/R4 defined a stage-pooled index $d_i = -z(\text{pred\_loss}_i) -
z(\text{recovery}_i)$ over each stage's cells (pooled across models, then
split per model; A6 verified the pooling). On the three post-gate stages,
LeJEPA is consistently and significantly \emph{decoupled}:
$d = +1.15$ (stage 1b --- the gate rerun batch, whose L0-gate and L1-bridge
cells both read out by ridge $R^2$, so the pooled standardization is
metric-consistent), $+1.47$ (L4), $+1.44$ (L2t) --- it predicts the next
state better than stage-average while recovering the ground-truth latent worse.
InfoNCE mirrors the opposite sign ($-1.15$, $-1.92$, $-1.61$); recon sits near
zero ($+0.17$ to $+0.46$). The instrument quantifies, on known ground truth,
the ``world model that predicts without understanding'' failure that
motivates identifiability analysis.

\section{Power analysis: the bars were fair}
\label{sec:power}
A null is only interpretable if the study could have detected the pre-registered
effect. For the paired per-seed deltas ($n{=}5$, $\alpha{=}0.05$ two-sided,
observed within-condition sd ${\approx}0.05$), the minimum detectable effect at
80\% power is \textbf{0.081}. The frozen H1b bar ($0.2$) had essentially 100\%
power; even the \emph{observed} $+0.113$ had 99\% power; the H1a bar ($0.3$)
and H2 bar ($0.1$) likewise $>99$\%. Power was never the reason for the nulls:
the effects are genuinely smaller than the a priori bars. Conversely, the tight
CIs (half-width ${\pm}0.062$ at the observed sd) mean the directional JEPA
effect is measured precisely --- it is a real $\approx0.11$ advantage, not an
unresolved near-zero.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What is new.}
A measurement contribution: the first discrete ladder for JEPA identifiability
with known ground truth, under a protocol that lets the nulls speak. Three
pre-registered hypotheses came back null (H1a same-metric, H1b at bar, H2),
and the nulls are informative: discreteness of the generator is not a distinct
failure mode beyond readout quantization; composition gives the predictive
objective a real but sub-bar advantage; SIGReg's cluster blur is sub-bar under
the corrected instrument. The decoupling index is a new, cheap instrument for
a failure mode --- predicting well while recovering badly --- that prior work
discusses but does not measure on known latents.

\paragraph{What we got wrong, and what the machinery did about it.}
Three instrument failures on our side were found and fixed by audit, not by
peeking: (i)~the A4 L2m control matched mean marginal entropy but not the joint
state space (27 vs.\ 8 states) --- fixed by L2mH; the effect estimate was
unchanged, which is evidence of robustness rather than of nothing;
(ii)~H1a's first scoring compared accuracy to $R^2$ on different scales,
manufacturing a ``partial pass'' --- re-scored same-metric, it is a null;
(iii)~H2's purity metric sat at the floor for both models --- AMI sees the
predicted direction. Each correction was registered as an amendment before the
corrected runs, and each corrected result is on disk. The pre-registered bars
were never moved; the power analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:power}) confirms they were
reachable.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(1)~$n{=}5$ seeds: adequate for the effect sizes here (Sec.~\ref{sec:power})
but small for the high-variance L3 AMI arm. (2)~The L3 grammar is one
construction; Turkish morphology (L4) is real but small. (3)~Linear-probe
recovery is the theorem's readout but not the only one; AMI, purity, and
nonlinear probes disagree in places, and the disagreement is itself
instructive. (4)~All worlds are static paired-view generators; action
conditioning is the controlled case (see \citet{controlledWM2026}).
(5)~Discrete worlds tested up to $S{=}10$ and 3-bit grammars; the regime
beyond is untested. (6)~H1b's frozen bar ($\Delta{>}0.2$ on accuracy) may be
large for the mechanism; a future pre-registration could size an effect from
the now-published $+0.11$ estimate.

\paragraph{What is open.}
The composition signal deserves a dedicated study: stronger grammars, deeper
rule hierarchies, and effect-size bars sized from this paper's estimate.
The decoupling index should be tested under action-conditioned objectives.
And the honest-null template --- frozen bars, powered nulls, instrument
audits as amendments --- is, we hope, a contribution to how self-supervised
identifiability gets measured as it moves beyond Gaussians.
