\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The formal identifiability guarantee for Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architectures (JEPAs) is Gaussian-bound: \citet{klindt2026lejepa} prove in
Lean~4 that a LeJEPA objective with sketched Gaussian regularization (SIGReg)
recovers latent states linearly (up to rotation) \emph{if and only if} the
latents are Gaussian under a stationary OU transition. Real language,
perception, and world states are neither Gaussian nor continuous --- they are
categorical, rule-governed, and discrete. This paper maps \emph{how and where}
identifiability breaks as the latent regime leaves the theorem's boundary. We
build a procedurally generated ladder of five worlds from continuous Gaussian
(OU) through discretized Gaussian, Markov-chain categories, a rule-governed
vowel-harmony grammar, and a Turkish orthography (caret-rule) lexicon --- each
with known ground-truth latents --- and train three objectives (LeJEPA/SIGReg,
input-space reconstruction, InfoNCE) plus VICReg under a frozen, pre-registered
protocol (amendments A1--A5, append-only). Findings (honest, including nulls):
\textbf{[to be finalized when stages 5--6 complete]}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and the theorem's boundary}
The identifiability question for self-supervised world models: when does the
embedding recover the true latent state, not merely a statistically convenient
code? \citet{klindt2026lejepa} give the sharpest formal answer to date ---
Gaussian latents are necessary and sufficient for linear recovery under their
objective (Thm.~5.1 forward/backward, Lean-4 verified). Fig.~4b of that paper
shows the empirical signature: recovery peaks at the Gaussian shape
($\alpha=2$) and degrades on both sides (heavy tails, uniform).

Two facts make the boundary scientifically live. First, the continuous
extension is already being mapped by adjacent work (\citet{arya2026};
see \S~6 related work). Second, \emph{the regime that actual data lives in} ---
discreteness, categories, rules, orthography --- is where the theorem is silent
by construction: its assumptions are Gaussian. We therefore ask, with the
theorem's own instruments (known ground-truth latents, linear-probe readout),
what happens below the boundary.

\section{Reproduction (sanity gate, F1)}
\textbf{Official gate: PASS.} Under A3 operationalization (2-D spiral mixing,
$\lambda=10$ SIGReg for JEPA, $\tau=0.3$ for InfoNCE), the Fig-4b signature
reproduces: JEPA ridge $R^2$ peaks at $\alpha=2$ (mean 0.85 over 3 seeds;
seed 0: 0.94), rising monotonically $0.04\to0.85$ through the heavy-tail region
and decaying to $\sim0.50$ at the uniform extreme. InfoNCE also peaks at
$\alpha=2$ (0.965) with the wider plateau Fig-4b notes; reconstruction
plateaus (autoencoder, expected; excluded from the gate under A3).
\textbf{Honest negative retained:} the original A2 configuration (quadratic
mixing, dim 8, $\lambda=1$) failed monotonically (uniform 0.885 $>$ Gaussian
0.815); root cause isolated by experiment --- the quadratic map's unbounded
scale makes it uninvertible for the small encoder at every $\alpha$, so the
model never enters the theorem's positive regime. Instrument corrected, not
selectively reported (stages 1 and 1b both on disk).

\subsection{L4 v2: Turkish caret rule (stage 3 re-run)}
The v1 world crashed (constant latent column, no negative class) and leaked
$z$ into $x$. v2 (A4): matched negative class, surface-only observations.
15/15 cells run clean, 0 errors. JEPA acc $0.60$--$0.79$ (mean $\sim0.71$)
--- far below the v1 ceiling, i.e., the redesigned world discriminates.

\subsection{VICReg (A5): the faithful fourth objective}
VICReg (variance--invariance--covariance, standard $25/25/1$ weights) shows
\textbf{no sharp $\alpha{=}2$ peak} on the L0 gate: recovery rises to a
plateau $0.975$--$0.987$ from $\alpha{\geq}1$ and stays there through the
uniform extreme ($0.975$ at $\alpha{=}32$). Its variance hinge + covariance
decorrelation whiten the embedding but do not \emph{Gaussianize} it, so
bounded uniform latents remain linearly recoverable --- the A5-predicted
absence of a SIGReg-style distortion. On the discrete ladder VICReg mirrors
the non-predictive objectives: no structure benefit at L3 ($0.714$ vs L2m
$0.881$, $\Delta{-}0.167$), collapse along the L2 $S$-dial ($0.84\to0.45$),
L4 $0.730$. Descriptive only (no H-test, per A5).

\section{Decoupling: predicting well without understanding (F3)}
Within-stage z-scored index (A5/R4): JEPA shows large positive $d$ on the
clean single-world stages --- $+1.15$ (L0 gate), $+1.47$ (L4), $+1.44$
(L2t) --- i.e., it predicts the next state better than stage-average while
recovering the latent worse; contrastive mirrors negative on the same stages
($-1.15$, $-1.92$, $-1.61$). Descriptive; plotted in F3.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{What is new.} A measurement contribution: the first ladder into the
discrete/symbolic regime with the theorem's own instruments, plus the
decoupling instrument (prediction vs state recovery on known ground truth).
\textbf{What is honest.} H1b at the pre-registered effect size is a null
(directional only); L4-v2 shows real but bounded recovery. The discrete
collapse (L1/L2) is steep, and at $S{=}10$ the discrete recovery falls
$>0.3$ below its moment-matched continuous twin (H1a partial pass), while at
$S{<}10$ the gaps are real but below the frozen bar (honest partial). \textbf{What is open.} Controlled/action-conditioned
discrete identifiability (\citet{controlledWM2026}); entropy-matched
bounds at larger state counts.
